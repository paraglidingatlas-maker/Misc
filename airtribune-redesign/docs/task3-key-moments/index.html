SRS Tapalpa 2026 · Task 3 · 10 Nov · for review

How Task 3 was won

The 58.7 km race to goal, read from all 106 official tracklogs at one fix per second. The big gaps opened in the last 6 km. Varga, Quintanilla and Luebberke found a 2.0–2.4 m/s climb 1.2 km north of the main gaggle. They left it three minutes earlier at the same height and finished three minutes ahead.

Draft for the owner to check before anything is published. Times are local (UTC−6). "Behind" means time behind the fastest pilot at the same distance to go. Distance to go is the shortest remaining route from each pilot's own position.

Key moments

12:15Start, 57.8 km to go

A clean line, no start advantage

The field was packed on the start cylinder edge at the gate: 80 of 87 goal pilots crossed within 60 s. Varga was 50 m inside the edge at 12:15:00 and crossed at 12:15:05 at 2,753 m, Quintanilla a second later at 2,815 m. With one gate and everyone timed from 12:15, the race was decided in the air.

Evidence: SSS exit times from the IGC fixes; all top-20 pilots crossed between 12:15:01 and 12:15:38.

12:15–12:43Leg to TP2

Quintanilla and Varga lead out by barely turning

Quintanilla flew the leg in 27:22, the fastest of the day, and Varga in 27:54. They circled for only 4:18 and 6:04, about a fifth of the leg. Belcourt, Nazander, Desmond and Castro circled 12–17 minutes and tagged TP2 about seven minutes later (12:49:51). Desmond, Belcourt and Castro then flew the three fastest TP2–TP3 legs and were back with the leaders by 13:05.

Leading out paid in points: Quintanilla finished 32 s after Luebberke but placed 2nd (979.8 to 977.9). The likely reason is lead-out points.

12:56–13:0335–34 km to go

Varga's bad patch

After TP2 Varga got low. At 12:58:30 he took a 0.9 m/s climb from 1,810 m, about 460 m above the ground, and worked it for 4:42. At 34 km to go he was 6 minutes behind the fastest pilot there. Takats led the race from 42 to 28 km to go.

13:05–13:1032 km to go

The big climb of the day

The whole lead gaggle found a climb averaging about 4 m/s over 30 s at 32 km to go. The leaders left it at about 2,680 m at 13:08:30. Varga came in later and lower, stayed until 13:10:15 and topped out at 2,767 m, with a 3.0 m/s average over 4:27. The climb erased his deficit: from 13:04 to 13:12 he gained 4.5 minutes on the fastest pilot.

13:14–13:2728–19 km to go

Varga skips the gaggle's thermal

At 27.7 km to go the leaders (Quintanilla, Luebberke, Spancerski, Diez, Arriaga, Desmond, Takats) stopped again. They climbed 2.2–2.5 m/s to about 2,850 m between 13:13:30 and 13:16:30. Varga glided past about 300 m away at 2,335 m without turning and led the race from 13:14:50. His lead was largest at 13:17:00, 1.6 km.

He paid for it. He was 500 m lower and needed three weak climbs between 13:18 and 13:27 (1.8, 0.8 and 1.4 m/s). By 13:26:40 the gaggle was level with him, everyone at 19.2–19.5 km to go, and he was about 150 m lower. He still tagged TP3 first, at 13:26:22.

Check against the 3D replay. The replay's race order shows Varga 2.7 km clear at 13:26:40. That is a measuring artefact. The replay measures every pilot to one fixed point on TP3's 31 km cylinder, so the gaps jump when the first pilot tags it. Measured from each pilot's own position, the gaggle was level with him.
13:13–13:3126–24 km to go

Low save for Sigel and McClurg

Sigel (SUI) and McClurg (USA) dropped out of the back of the lead group. At 13:21 they were about 10 m and 100 m above the ground respectively; the ground model is approximate. Both climbed out: Sigel at 2.4 m/s to 2,552 m, and McClurg at 1.7 m/s after circling for 9:37. Sigel lost about 14 minutes between 25 and 22 km to go. Both still made goal, 19th and 20th.

13:27–14:00TP3 to TP4

Pachura's fastest leg

Pachura flew TP3–TP4 in 25:55, two minutes faster than anyone in the top 20, with only 3:06 of circling. He started the leg about 10 minutes down after a slow first half of the race, so it won back time without moving him far: he finished 17th.

13:42–13:5911 km to go

Siegel and Nazander go long

Siegel and Nazander were four minutes behind the leaders. They climbed together to 3,190 m 11.4 km out and set off on final glide from there, with about 1,840 m over goal (they needed a glide ratio of 6.2). They flew it at 57–59 km/h. They passed the main gaggle while it sat in its last thermal, finished at 13:59:35 and 13:59:22, and placed 6th and 7th. Their gap to the fastest pilot shrank from 4 minutes to 20 s.

Nicol flew the same plan from 11.8 km but arrived 605 m over goal, the highest of the top 20.

13:49–13:565 km to go

The winning move: the better last thermal

At 13:50 almost the whole lead group was 5 km from goal at 1,780–1,910 m, and they split between two thermals 1.2 km apart:

  • North: Varga, Quintanilla and Luebberke, 2.0–2.4 m/s. They left at 13:52:50 with about 800 m over goal.
  • South: Spancerski, Diez, Arriaga, Forgo, Alvarado, Castro and Takats, 0.8–1.2 m/s. They left at 13:55:50 at the same height.

Time gained: about 3 minutes. The north group finished 13:58:08–13:59:14 and took 1st to 3rd. The south group finished 14:00:55–14:01:55.

Belcourt skipped both thermals, glided on lower and stopped for a short climb 2.6 km out. He arrived 158 m over goal at 13:59:26 for 4th.

Call to check. The south group's glides were flown at a glide ratio of about 11. At 13:52–13:53 they had about 520 m over goal with 5 km to go, so on paper they could have left then. At their climb rate the extra 300 m took them 4–6 minutes, and they arrived 250–400 m over goal. Varga also arrived 364 m high. A field this uniform suggests they all expected sink or headwind near the landing field. The tracks can't show wind on the glide.
13:56:331.5 km to go

Quintanilla turns once too many

With 410 m over goal and 1.5 km to go, Quintanilla circled for 73 s at 1.9 m/s. He arrived 545 m over goal, the highest of the top five. At his previous ground speed (52 km/h) he would have finished at about 13:58:18 instead of 13:59:14. That would still have left him about 10 s behind Varga, but ahead of Luebberke on time.

14:00–14:05Goal

Takats and Andreliunas circle outside goal

After tagging TP4, both pilots circled 300–450 m from the goal centre, just outside the 200 m goal cylinder. Takats circled for 3:43 and descended from about 335 m to 45 m over goal before he went in, at 14:04:52. Flying straight in would have taken him about 50 s, so this cost him about 3.5 minutes: about 12th on time instead of 16th. Andreliunas lost about 2 minutes the same way (14th).

Inference, not fact. One explanation is that their instruments had a larger goal radius and showed them in goal. Confirm with the pilots or the organisers before saying it publicly.

What the data can and can't show

  • Wind is estimated from how ground speed changes around each circle, not measured. It was about 7 km/h from the east before the start and about 10 km/h from the south-east on leg 1, then light and variable (median under 5 km/h). Nothing is known about wind on the glides.
  • Airspeed and speed bar are not recorded, so any bar use is a guess from ground speed.
  • Reasons are inferences. The tracks show where, when and how high, not why. The calls marked "to check" need a pilot's eye or a word with the pilots.
  • Height above ground uses the ground elevation stored in each IGC file. It is coarse near terrain.
  • Distances use a flat local grid. The task comes out 58.56 km against the official 58.7 km, about 0.25 % short. One goal pilot's file has no start crossing, so the field used here is 87 pilots.
